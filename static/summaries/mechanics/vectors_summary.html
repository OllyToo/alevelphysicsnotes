Scalars vs. vectors

ScalarVector
speed, mass, energyvelocity, force, momentum
distance, time, powerdisplacement, moment, field strength
  • Scalar: magnitude only.
  • Vector: magnitude and direction — shown as +/−, an angle from a reference line, or a compass point.
Distance vs. displacement: distance is the total path length; displacement is the straight line from start to end. A full circular lap has distance = πd but displacement = 0.

Adding vectors in 1D

For vectors acting along the same line, assign consistent + and − directions, then add algebraically. The sign of the resultant tells you its direction — it doesn't matter which direction you call positive, as long as you're consistent throughout.

Vectors at right angles

The resultant is the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle formed by the two vectors.

Drawn nose-to-tail, two perpendicular vectors form a right-angled triangle with the resultant as the hypotenuse:

FR = √(F₁²+F₂²)    θ = tan⁻¹(opposite/adjacent)

Resolving a vector into components

The reverse process: splitting one vector into two perpendicular components.

Any vector can be split into two components at right angles using trigonometry — essential for forces on inclined planes and projectile motion:

H = S cosθ  (adjacent)    V = S sinθ  (opposite)

Scale diagrams (non-right-angle vectors)

The completed parallelogram — resultant is the diagonal from the origin.

For vectors at any other angle, draw a parallelogram of forces:

  • Draw both vectors to scale from a common origin.
  • Measure the angle between them and complete the parallelogram.
  • Draw the diagonal from the origin — this is the resultant. Measure its length (convert via the scale) and its angle with a protractor.

Getting full marks

You must draw the diagram for full marks in the exam, even if the sine or cosine rule could give the same numerical answer — drawing accurately is a required skill in its own right, not just a means to an answer.

Use a sharp pencil, a ruler and a protractor. Choose as large a scale as reasonably fits the page, to keep measurement errors small.

Exam essentials

Key equations

  • FR = √(F₁²+F₂²)
  • θ = tan⁻¹(opp/adj)
  • H = S cosθ, V = S sinθ

Choosing a method

  • Vectors at 90°: use trigonometry (Pythagoras + tan).
  • Vectors at other angles: scale diagram (or sine/cosine rule, but draw the diagram too if asked).

Common slips

  • Keep the sign convention consistent throughout a 1D addition — don't flip which direction is positive halfway through.
  • Scale diagrams need a sharp pencil, ruler and protractor for marks — a rough sketch isn't enough.
  • Be clear which side (adjacent/opposite) you're resolving relative to before applying sin or cos.
